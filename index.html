FindingHome categories (bed and bath, furniture and decor) and sports bring people back most, about 1 in 40. Gadgets and accessories bring back the fewest. The nudge should recommend from the category the customer already bought, where a follow-up purchase is natural.

5. The experiment: a nudge before the moment passes

Primary metric

  • Second order on a later day, within 90 days of the first

Secondary

  • Second order within 180 days
  • Value of the second order
  • Message opens and clicks (leading signal)

Guardrails

  • Unsubscribe and complaint rate below [x]%
  • Review score on second orders not lower than first
  • Contribution per treated customer not negative (phase 2)

Two phases, cheapest first

Phase 1: does a reminder alone work?

Control: nothing, as today. Treatment: a message on day 21 with three products from the same category and seller. Costs almost nothing, so any lift pays.

Phase 2: is free shipping worth it?

Only if phase 1 lifts repeat orders. Reminder against reminder plus free shipping on the second order. Free shipping is paid on every second order, including the ones that would have happened anyway, so it must earn its cost. Use the calculator.

6. How long will it run, and does it pay?

Defaults come from the data. Change anything. Two-sided test, 5% significance.

Risks and how to handle them

  • Who owns the customer. Olist sells through large marketplaces, which may own the email address. If Olist cannot message buyers, run the same test as a card in the parcel with a QR code, randomised by seller dispatch batch.
  • Small numbers. At a 1.37% baseline, only big lifts are detectable in reasonable time. The calculator shows why the test targets a 50% lift and runs about 5 to 6 months including the 90-day window.
  • Novelty and timing. Day 21 is a choice from the data (a quarter of returners come back within 22 days). A later follow-up test could compare day 14 and day 45.
  • Cannibalisation. Some treated customers would have come back anyway. That is exactly what the control group measures, and why the voucher cost is spread over all treated second orders.

Limits

  • Data from 2016 to August 2018, and customers are matched by Olist's unique customer ID, which may miss people who used a different account.
  • Second orders on the same day as the first (30% of all second orders) are treated as one split basket, not a return.
  • The segments are correlations. A bad first order and not returning go together, but other things may explain both.
  • Nothing here has been tested. The experiment is a design, and every lift in the calculator is an input, not a result.
